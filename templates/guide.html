ApexFlow Guide

A practical walk-through of how the Heatseeker matrix, the projection chart, and the probability tools fit together — and a checklist of setups you can hunt with them.

What is GEX?

Gamma exposure (GEX) is the amount of stock dealers must buy or sell at each strike to stay neutral. When traders buy options, dealers take the other side and hedge by trading the underlying. How much they have to hedge depends on the option's gamma — and gamma is concentrated near the strike price.

Sum that hedging requirement across every open interest contract at every strike and you get a landscape of forced flow: levels where dealers will buy stock if price falls below, levels where they'll sell if price rises through. That landscape behaves like mechanical support and resistance.

Why it matters. GEX walls don't predict the future, they describe the plumbing. Like seeing where speed bumps are on a road. Price still does what news/flow makes it do — but when it gets near a big GEX wall, the road changes.

Color spectrum

Each cell in the Heatseeker matrix shows the net dollar GEX at that strike. Color encodes both sign (positive vs. negative) and magnitude:

Bright yellow Strong positive GEX. Pinning / absorbing — price slows, gets sticky.
Green Mid positive. Mild support / resistance.
Dim Near-zero GEX. Air zone — price moves through fast.
Cyan Mid negative. Mild acceleration zone.
Bright purple Strong negative GEX. Acceleration — price expands volatility through this level.

The continuous red line across each pane is current spot price. The thicker / brighter the cell, the more material the level. Dim italic cells are air zones where there's almost no dealer hedging — price has a clear runway between meaningful walls.

Magnet strikes

The strike with the largest absolute GEX in scope is the primary magnet — surrounded by a yellow halo. The next four largest are secondary magnets with a thinner yellow border.

  • Positive magnets (yellow) behave like glue. Price tends to drift toward them, then chop around them. Good fade / mean-reversion territory while in regime.
  • Negative magnets (purple) behave like trampolines. Price often slows on approach, then accelerates away. Be careful pressing trades into them in either direction — moves get explosive.

The footer of each pane shows the primary magnet strike and the gamma flip price so you can read the structure at a glance.

Air zones

Strikes where dealers have minimal hedging needs (less than ~10% of the largest level) render dim and italic. Price moves through these zones quickly because nothing is mechanically slowing it down.

An air zone between spot and the next magnet is one of the most useful patterns in the matrix — it tells you the path of least resistance to the next decision point.

Gamma flip

The gamma flip is the price level where cumulative dealer GEX crosses zero.

  • Above the flip, dealers are net long gamma → they lean against the move (sell rallies, buy dips). Markets feel sticky, ranges hold, intraday vol is suppressed.
  • Below the flip, dealers are net short gamma → they lean with the move (sell dips, buy rallies). Markets feel slippery, breakouts work, intraday vol expands.

A regime change at the flip is one of the most reliable setups: positions that worked above the flip often stop working below it, and vice versa.

Stacked levels

When two adjacent strikes have opposite-sign GEX of meaningful size, the structure becomes a loaded spring. Two patterns:

Reverse stack bearish bias

Positive GEX over negative GEX. Price sits on a yellow shelf, but if it slips below into the purple, the floor falls out — there's no support all the way to the next magnet.

Watch for: sharp candle close below the upper strike on volume.

Spring stack bullish bias

Negative GEX under positive GEX. Price compresses below a yellow ceiling. A break above flips the magnet from resistance to support and momentum tends to extend.

Watch for: a clean reclaim of the upper strike with follow-through.

Expiry layers

Different option expirations exert different kinds of force. The toolbar has three preset layers plus All:

  • Front (0–2 DTE) — drives intraday speed. Where 0DTE traders pile in. Magnets here often hold for hours, then evaporate after expiry.
  • Middle (7–30 DTE) — drives weekly drift. The "cleanest" gamma view for swing trading. Less noise from 0DTE chop.
  • Back (45+ DTE) — drives structural gravity. The longer-term institutional positioning. Slow to change but heavy.
  • All — blended view; useful for context but the levels can be diluted.

Pro tip: when Front and Middle agree on a magnet (same strike shows up bright in both), that level is a high-conviction zone.

Projection chart

The chart at the top of Heatseeker shows recent price candles, with all the gamma walls drawn as horizontal lines and a forward probability cone projecting the price distribution to your selected horizon.

What's drawn

  • Yellow lines — positive GEX walls. Likely magnets / pin levels.
  • Purple lines — negative GEX walls. Likely accelerators / breakout pivots.
  • Brightest yellow line — primary magnet. Each line label shows the wall's $GEX and the touch probability.
  • Cyan median path — the expected price drift under risk-neutral assumptions (median of the lognormal distribution).
  • Cyan cone (P5/P25/P50/P75/P95) — the percentile envelope of where price could be at the horizon. The wider the cone, the higher the implied vol or longer the horizon.

Toggle TradingView Pro to swap in a full TradingView chart with the standard indicators (BB, EMA, RSI, VWAP, Volume, Pivots) and TV's drawing tools — useful when you want to draw your own levels or use other studies.

Probability table

The right-side table lists each gamma wall with two probabilities, computed from the lognormal price distribution under current ATM IV:

  • Touch % — probability that price reaches this strike at any time before the horizon. Computed via the reflection principle on geometric Brownian motion.
  • Above % — probability that price finishes above this strike at the horizon. This is the standard N(d₂) implied terminal probability.

The bar behind each value is a quick visual indicator of magnitude. Touch is always ≥ Above — the price might get there and come back.

Probabilities use the implied volatility surface as their input. They are risk-neutral, not real-world. They're best read as "what the options market thinks today" — useful for sizing and timing, not as a crystal ball.

Expected move

The sidebar shows the 1σ expected dollar move for your horizon: spot × IV × √(t). Roughly two-thirds of the time price will finish inside this range; one-third of the time it won't.

Compare it to the gamma walls:

  • If a strong magnet sits well inside the 1σ range, expect price to gravitate there.
  • If the next magnet sits just past the 1σ band, breaking through it has lasting weight.
  • If price already pierced 1σ early in the day, the rest of the move tends to be reversion (fade) unless a hard catalyst is driving it.

Recognising configurations

The matrix and chart together surface a handful of repeatable gamma configurations. Each describes where dealer hedging flow would come from and what that mechanically does to realised volatility around a level. Read the gamma flip first — the same strike layout means opposite things on either side of it.

These are structural descriptions, not recommendations. ApexFlow does not tell you what to do with them, and the mechanism described only holds while the dealer-positioning assumption behind GEX holds — see Caveats.

Above the flip · long-gamma regime

Magnet pin range

Spot is near a strong yellow magnet with no opposing wall close by.

Mechanism: positive dealer gamma means hedges are sold into strength and bought into weakness, compressing realised vol around the level. Price tends to chop rather than trend while this holds.

Positive magnet above spot resistance

Price runs toward an above-spot positive magnet on weak follow-through.

Mechanism: approaching the strike grows dealer long gamma, so hedging flow leans against the move. The level acts as static resistance until the gamma there is exhausted or rolls off.

Positive magnet below spot support

Price drops toward a below-spot positive magnet without a catalyst.

Mechanism: the mirror image — dealer hedging buys weakness into the strike, so the level tends to absorb downside in the absence of news.

Below the flip · short-gamma regime

Air zone expansion

Spot has cleared a magnet with several strikes of near-zero GEX above (or below).

Mechanism: with little gamma to hedge, dealer flow neither damps nor amplifies much — there is simply no structural brake between here and the next magnet, so moves travel further per unit of order flow.

Negative under positive unstable

Negative GEX sits beneath positive GEX and price is at the boundary.

Mechanism: the hedging regime changes character as price crosses. Below the boundary dealers chase moves; above it they lean against them. Volatility is regime-dependent right at the crossing.

Positive over negative unstable

Positive GEX sits above negative GEX and spot fails the upper shelf.

Mechanism: losing the shelf moves price into territory where dealer hedging runs with the move rather than against it, so realised vol expands on the downside.

Either regime

Multi-layer alignment persistent

The same strike is a magnet in both the Front (0–2 DTE) and Middle (7–30 DTE) layers.

Mechanism: the level is reinforced by two different time-horizon flows, so it survives individual expirations — dealers re-stack there as near-dated contracts roll off. Structurally the most durable kind of level.

Touch / above divergence two questions

A strike shows high Touch % but low Above %.

Mechanism: these answer different questions. Touch is a path probability (does price tag the level at any point), Above is a terminal one (does it finish beyond). Touch is always the larger, and roughly double for a driftless barrier — a wide gap is normal geometry, not an anomaly.

Reading a symbol, in order

A sequence for interpreting the display — what to look at and in what order.

  1. Where is spot relative to the zero-gamma level? That sets which regime everything below is read in.
  2. Is the flip bracketed? If the panel says “no flip within ±25%”, there is no crossing in the searched range and the regime label is the whole answer.
  3. How trustworthy is the ATM IV? A low confidence flag means the near-the-money strikes disagreed badly, and every probability below inherits that.
  4. Where is the primary magnet, and is it above or below spot?
  5. Is there an air zone between spot and the next magnet?
  6. Do the Front and Middle layers agree on the magnet?
  7. What is the 1σ expected move over your horizon, and where do the magnets sit relative to it?
  8. What does the probability table say — and are you reading Touch or Above?
  9. What is in the catalyst window — earnings, FOMC, CPI, OPEX? Positioning can reshape completely across an event.

Backtesting

There used to be a Backtest page here. It replayed three price-based scanners against historical bars, which is the weakest question this project can ask, and a browser form was never going to represent the real work honestly. Both now live in the CLI.

Scanner replay weak

Replays a scanner's boolean daily signal against price bars.

python main.py backtest pre-breakout --hold 5

Squeeze score the real test

Cross-sectional rank test with a permutation null, an overlap correction, and a coverage gate that refuses a verdict when most of the score cannot be reconstructed point-in-time.

python main.py backtest-squeeze --universe squeeze

Monte Carlo validation

Prices options, digitals and a barrier by simulation and compares each against closed form.

python main.py validate-mc

What the squeeze backtest actually found: no rank information (IC −0.049, inside the permutation null), but the top score decile produced a mean return of +14.5% against a median of −1.6%, and the rate of >20% moves rose from 7.2% to 13.0% across deciles. A right-tailed payoff that a rank statistic cannot represent — which is a different claim from "it works", and is written up in full in docs/methodology.md §10.

Caveats

  • Free data is delayed. The free yfinance feed is ~15-min delayed and doesn't include real-time flow / dark-pool prints. For live tape add a paid provider key to keys.py.
  • GEX is dealer-implied. It assumes typical dealer positioning (long calls, short puts). Real dealer books can differ; treat the levels as guides not gospel.
  • Probabilities are risk-neutral. They use the implied vol the market is currently pricing — they update as IV updates.
  • Gamma walls expire. 0DTE walls evaporate after the cash close. Re-check before each session.
  • This is research, not trading advice. Always paper-validate before risking capital.
